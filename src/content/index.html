Guess the most common answer, right in your browser

OpenNoillirk is a free daily game where the answer most people would give wins.

Every day OpenNoillirk brings ten prompts with 30 seconds each. Name anything that fits: the more people would give your answer, the more points you score and the more of the table's 100 people join you.

There is nothing to install and no account to make. Press Play above for today's ten, or open Unlimited for another run whenever you like. It works with a mouse and keyboard, and on a phone with the on-screen keyboard open.

How to play OpenNoillirk

One prompt, thirty seconds, and a crowd of a hundred people who join you when you guess what most of them would say.

OpenNoillirk starts you on the sky climb, where the same points lift our krill on three balloons instead of pulling a crowd. A #1 Answer in question 1 is 100 points and takes it to 30m; a perfect Daily of 2,500 points ends at 14.0 km. Rather play on the table? The start screen switches between the two.

Why the most common answer wins in OpenNoillirk

Same OpenNoillirk prompt, five answers. Tap one to see what it scores.

Every OpenNoillirk prompt has a board: our estimate, made in advance, of how 100 people would respond. The board's top five stand on the table as tiles with the crowd on them, and the #1 tile is the biggest. For “Name a capital city in Europe” they are Paris, London, Rome, Berlin and Madrid.

Then your OpenNoillirk figurine hops to your pick. Say and you land on the biggest tile, the #1 Answer: 100 points, and people walk over to join you. Say and you are right, but you stand on a small tile of your own beside the table: 10 points, and nobody joins you.

Every accepted guess scores something, so a quick one is never wasted. But in OpenNoillirk the #1 Answer is worth ten times an Off the Board one. Length, spelling and cleverness don't matter, only how many people would say it.

How OpenNoillirk scoring works

Each accepted answer falls into one of five bands. The band comes from the answer's place on that prompt's board, our estimate of how 100 people would answer, and it sets the base points.

Questions 3 to 5 of a OpenNoillirk Daily count twice, 6 to 8 three times and 9 and 10 four times, and every 25 points pulls one person to your home tile. For the thresholds, a worked example and how each title is earned, see how scoring works in detail.

Win the OpenNoillirk crowd

Every OpenNoillirk run starts with a hundred people on the table. The more people would give your answer, the more of them join you.

The crowd is 100 little tourists. After each round, one of them walks over to join you on your home tile for every 25 points you scored, and stays there with a ring in your colour. The number on your tile keeps count.

At the end of a OpenNoillirk run the result reads “You pulled 26 of 100”. Ten #1 Answers pull all 100: that is a perfect Daily. The people are what count: a challenge, its board and today's percentile all compare how many you pulled, and points only break a tie on the board.

OpenNoillirk tips: how to guess the most common answer

In most trivia you look for a right answer. In OpenNoillirk you look for the one most people would give, and three habits help.

Say your first thought

The first thing that comes to mind is usually everyone's, and that is the one you want. Don't overthink it. After every guess the OpenNoillirk result card shows “Our board Paris, London, Rome, Berlin, Madrid (est.)”, the board's top five, so each prompt teaches you where most people's minds go.

Pick the most famous one

The best-known member of a category is usually #1, so reach for the familiar, everyday one. For a moon in our solar system, The Moon is the #1 Answer, Titan is a Runner-up, and Adrastea is right but Off the Board, worth only 10 points.

Beat the clock

Thirty seconds go fast. Any accepted guess scores, while a perfect one still in your head when time runs out scores nothing. OpenNoillirk Unlimited is the place to practise, and after every run the answers review shows each prompt's top five from our board.

Share your OpenNoillirk result and challenge a friend

Guessing what most people say is more fun against someone you know, so every OpenNoillirk run ends with cards and a link made for that.

When a OpenNoillirk run ends, Share & challenge makes share cards and a short text. The text shows how many people you pulled, one coloured circle per question and your title, but none of your answers, so it never spoils the Daily for anyone.

Play the same prompts as your friends

Every Daily share, and every share of a 10- or 20-prompt Unlimited run, carries a challenge link. A friend who opens it plays the same prompts, and after each answer they see your figurine on the tile you chose, with your name on it. At the end they see where they landed on the challenge board of everyone who played that link.

When your friend shares, their link brings new players to the same board, so a whole group can play one challenge. It's an easy way to play with friends online: no account, just a random nickname such as Swift Otter that you can change before you send.

OpenNoillirk FAQ

Short answers to what people ask before their first OpenNoillirk run.

Is OpenNoillirk free?

Yes. OpenNoillirk is a free daily game with no account and no paywall, and Unlimited is free too. Start from the game at the top of this page.

Can I play OpenNoillirk on my phone?

Yes. OpenNoillirk runs in a phone browser, and the answer box stays above the on-screen keyboard for the whole run. Press Go, or your keyboard's Go key, to submit.

How long does a OpenNoillirk Daily take?

Ten prompts with up to 30 seconds each is at most five minutes of play, plus a few seconds of reveal after each guess. Go faster to finish sooner, or tap the table to speed up a reveal.

How is OpenNoillirk different from trivia games?

Most trivia games have one right answer and reward whoever finds it first. OpenNoillirk accepts many right answers and rewards the one most people would give. Knowing a lot helps, but thinking like everyone else wins.

What kind of prompts does OpenNoillirk ask?

Prompts come from everyday categories: places, food, music, books, sport and the human body. Each one names a category, such as a capital city in Europe or a Pixar film, and accepts many answers. Your job is to guess the one most people would say first.

How does OpenNoillirk decide which answer is #1?

Every prompt has a board: our estimate, made in advance, of how 100 people would respond, from the most common answer down to the tenth. Where your guess sits on that board decides its band. The board isn't a live count of other players, which is why it is marked (est.).

Why didn't OpenNoillirk accept my guess?

Each prompt has a prepared list of accepted answers. Common variants, plurals and small typos are recognised, but no list is complete, so try another while the clock runs. If you pass or run out of time, the result card has a “Should this count? Tell us” button for answers you think we missed, and every guess OpenNoillirk turns down is counted anonymously so we can add the ones that should count.

How do challenge links work?

Every share carries a short link like opennoillirk.com/c/k7Qm2x9A. Your friend plays the same prompts and sees your pick after each of theirs, and everyone who plays that link lands on one board. To make the board and today's percentile work we keep each finished run: your nickname, answers and scores, and a random id created in your browser. There is no account and no email.

Does OpenNoillirk save my progress?

Your Daily progress, streak and settings are saved in this browser when storage is available. They don't follow you to another device or a private window, and clearing this site's data removes them.

Can I see the whole OpenNoillirk board?

After each guess you see the top five of that prompt's board. The rest stays hidden so a prompt is still worth playing when it comes round again.

Is OpenNoillirk affiliated with Daily Games?

No. OpenNoillirk is an independently hosted adaptation using authorized Noillirk assets. It is not affiliated with Daily Games. The deployable source is available on GitHub.
